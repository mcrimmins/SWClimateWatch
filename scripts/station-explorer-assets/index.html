Daily temperature normal lines: Project-calculated 1991-2020 calendar-day averages use paired, unflagged high/low observations, with at least 20 valid years per calendar date. A centered 15-day smoother is applied only where every date in its cyclic calendar window has adequate support; the reported support is the minimum across that window, and eligible years can vary by date. February 29 interpolates adjacent reference days. Hover departures use this smoothed reference; the table retains its existing period-anomaly method. These are not official NOAA normals, and no missing observed temperatures are filled.

Daily temperature record lines: Dotted lines show the highest daily maximum and lowest daily minimum among earlier eligible years on that exact calendar date. Current observations and later years are excluded. References need 30 earlier observations, with the earliest qualifying report at least 30 calendar years before the plotted year; February 29 needs at least eight actual earlier leap-day reports with the same age requirement, never interpolated values. Missing, flagged and inverted daily temperatures are excluded. Hover identifies record values, all tied record years and the comparison sample. New/tied markers describe this eligible cached station or ThreadEx record, not certified official records; comparison ties allow 0.01 degree F for numerical precision.

Daily precipitation record table: Observed totals are compared with earlier eligible reports on the exact calendar date, using the same 30-year count/age requirements as temperature records and actual leap-day reports only. Missing, flagged, accumulated and invalid reports are excluded. Trace reports are shown as trace and contribute zero to historical comparisons; zero or trace-only reference samples never create tied wettest-day labels. Hover records are within the eligible cached station or ThreadEx history, not certified official records. The wettest day or three-day span within the selected period is a separate indicator.

Reading this preview

Precipitation comparisons need every day. When only the most recent one or two reports are missing (one for a 7-day period), ≥ marks the observed total as a provisional lower bound; no departure or rank is shown until the period is complete. Mean temperature can use at least 95% valid paired high/low days if no gap exceeds two consecutive days. At 98% or more it displays normally; at 95–<98%, * marks the partial current result, including its departure and rank. Dashed edges identify these and other cautions. Missing days are never filled with zero.

The 6- and 12-month options are rolling calendar periods ending on the displayed date; their day counts vary with the calendar. Departures compare the current period with at least 15 eligible corresponding periods from 1991–2020 (15–19 receive a caution). Percentiles and ranks compare one corresponding period per earlier year across the station’s usable record; at least 25 years are required (25–29 receive a caution). Historical precipitation periods must be complete; historical temperature periods use the same 95% rule. A higher percentile means a wetter or warmer period. Ties use a midpoint percentile.

The extremes tab shows the wettest day, wettest three consecutive days, highest high, lowest low, unusually hot days, unusually warm nights, and longest hot and dry spells within the selected period. Temperature thresholds are each station's centered-five-day 1991–2020 90th percentiles; a dry day has less than 0.04 inch of precipitation. Freeze nights (minimum temperature at or below 32°F) use the latest October–March cool season instead of the selected period. The freeze departure is the observed count minus the average count for matching season-to-date periods in at least 15 complete 1991–2020 seasons. Positive means more freeze nights than usual. All these metrics require complete, unflagged observations; missing values are not imputed. They are rolling-period indicators, not historical records or formal annual Climdex indices.
